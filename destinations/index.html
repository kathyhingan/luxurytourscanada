The approach

Three regions, known road by road.

Every private Canada tour we design begins with a region, not a package. Most of our journeys run through one of three areas: the BC coast around Tofino and Salt Spring Island, the Canadian Rockies from Jasper down to Banff and Yoho, and the Ontario lakeshore from Kingston to Essex County.

Each region has its own season, its own roads, and its own way of rewarding a slower pace. We state drive times honestly, plan around wildfire season rather than hoping, and hold a second option for every outdoor day, because mountain weather turns by the hour.

There is a seventh corner we add on request: Point Pelee and Essex County at the southernmost edge of mainland Canada, where the bird migration peaks in May and the monarchs pass through in September. Ask, and we will say plainly whether it fits your dates.

Where we go

Six destinations, each built as a journey.

None of these is a day trip bolted onto a city stay. Each one anchors a journey of its own, and every page states the season, the drive times, and what we would skip.

Planning notes

When to go, honestly.

The BC coast window

May and June bring grey whales and quiet roads, September and October the clearest water, and November to February the storm watching season people plan a whole trip around. April is the month we rarely build.

The Rockies window

Late June to early October for open roads and long light, with the golden larches in mid September. Parkway fuel and services run May to mid October, and winter builds here are aurora trips, not road trips.

The lakeshore window

May to October, with the wine harvest late September into October and the Point Pelee migration in May. January is its own season: the icewine harvest in Niagara, picked at night below minus eight.

How far ahead

Summer Rockies journeys are planned between January and March, and the best tables and cabins book first. Autumn trips are booked in summer. Winter journeys, from storm season to icewine, are planned through autumn.

How long to stay

Seven to fourteen nights for one region done properly. A journey that crosses from the Pacific to the mountains and on to the lakeshore wants eighteen to twenty one nights, and rushing it wastes it.

Weather and wildfire

Mountain weather turns by the hour, so every outdoor day carries a second option. Wildfire season is planned around rather than hoped away, and we say plainly when your dates land in the April mud weeks.

How we design

Appointments first, then the map.

A region is only as good as its specific days, so we design from the appointments outward: the boat that leaves the harbour before the fleet, the cellar where the winemaker still pours, the table that is yours for the afternoon. The map fills in around them, at drive times we have done ourselves.

The whole lakeshore from Kingston to Essex County has been ridden on a bicycle rather than driven past, and the mountain roads are known in both directions, in the cold months as well as the warm ones. That is the standard every journey here is built to.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time of year for a private Canada tour?

It depends on the region. The Rockies are best from late June to early October. The BC coast is at its finest in May and June and from September into October, with storm season from November to February. The Ontario lakeshore runs May to October, with icewine harvest in January. Tell us your month and we will tell you honestly which region suits it.

How long should a private Canada tour be?

Seven to fourteen days for one region done properly, and eighteen to twenty-one days for a shape that crosses from the Pacific to the mountains and on to the lakeshore. Distance is the product here, so a rushed Canada is a wasted one.

Do you design journeys across all of Canada?

We build journeys in the three regions we know personally: the BC coast, the Canadian Rockies, and the Ontario lakeshore. If your wish list sits outside those areas, we will say so and point you to someone better placed, rather than designing a journey we cannot stand behind.
