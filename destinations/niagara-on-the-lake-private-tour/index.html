The region

Cellars with real age, at the mouth of the river.

This is the oldest wine region in the country, and it behaves like a European appellation: a heritage town where the Niagara River meets Lake Ontario, cellars with real age on them, and vines grown on the benches of the escarpment above. The town was the first capital of Upper Canada, and the main street still carries the stone of that period. The falls are twenty minutes away, which is close enough for a morning and far enough to leave behind by lunch.

The difference a private journey makes here is appointments. The visit that stays with guests is the one with the person who made the wine, in a cellar rather than a tasting room, at an hour when the room is quiet. We book those first, build the day around them, and drive, so nobody at the table has to count glasses. Icewine is the other reason people come: picked by hand in the dark at temperatures below minus eight, and if your journey lands in January you can be there while the region runs on harvest.

What to know before you go

Getting there

Fly to Toronto, and the region is about an hour and a half by road. Your guide and private vehicle collect you in the city, so the first vineyard view arrives before you have thought about the drive.

Best season

Late September to mid October brings harvest, colour, and the quietest good weather of the year. May to October runs on long evenings and theatre. January is the region's other self, built on icewine.

The icewine window

Icewine grapes are picked by hand at night when the temperature falls below minus eight. That can be December, January or February, and the growers decide on the day. We build a winter journey around a window rather than a date, and confirm the timing as it firms up.

Appointments

Tasting rooms fill on weekends and through the harvest. The visit worth making is the appointment with a person rather than a pour, so we book two or three of those first and let the rest of the day fall around them.

The parkway and the falls

The Niagara Parkway links the town to the falls in about twenty minutes. We drive it in the direction the light is best, take the falls early or late rather than at midday, and keep the route for the return trip when the day runs long.

Pacing and care

A driver stays with you for the whole day, which is the point of doing this region privately. We recommend travel medical insurance for guests arriving from outside Canada and say so in writing, and the itinerary states the walking at every stop honestly.

How we design this journey

Appointments first, then the town

We book the cellar appointments before we plan anything else, because the visit that makes the day is the one with a person rather than a pour. From there the shape is simple: two visits, one long lunch, and the evening wherever the light is best. The town is a short walk at either end, and the falls are an easy decision once the cellar times are fixed.

Harvest and festival dates move with the weather, so we check the current vintage and the growers' timing before every winter journey, and we keep a second appointment in reserve for any day the first one shifts. Everything here has been done first, in the cold as well as in the warm months: the cellar stairs, the table on the estate, and the river road in January.

Frequently asked questions

Do you drive between the wineries on a Niagara private tour?

Yes. A private vehicle and driver stay with you for the day, which is the whole point of doing this region privately: nobody at the table has to count glasses, and the schedule bends when a visit runs long.

When is icewine season in Niagara-on-the-Lake?

The harvest is in January, picked by hand at night when the temperature falls below minus eight. The festivals follow the harvest, so a winter journey here is built around that window and confirmed against the growers' timing.

How far is Niagara-on-the-Lake from Toronto?

About an hour and a half by road from the city, which makes it the easiest wine region in Canada to build into a journey, and the simplest day retreat for a corporate group.
