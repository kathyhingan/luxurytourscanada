Niagara-on-the-Lake Private Tour: Winery Visits with the Winemaker

Canada's oldest wine region, an hour from Toronto, with cellars that will open a private tasting for the right appointment and icewine harvested on the coldest nights of January.

Why Niagara-on-the-Lake for private travel

This is the oldest wine region in the country, and it behaves like a European appellation: a heritage town at the mouth of the Niagara River, cellars with real age on them, and a winter harvest that exists nowhere else in Canada. Icewine is picked at night, by hand, at temperatures below minus eight, and if your journey is in January you can be there for it.

The difference a private tour makes here is appointments. We book the visits where the person who made the wine takes you through it, at the times the tasting rooms are quiet, and we drive, so nobody at the table has to count glasses.

Key experiences

A wine day with a driver, and a winter one to remember

Cellar appointments

Two or three visits rather than five, chosen for what you actually drink, each with the winemaker or the cellar master rather than a host reading a card.

Icewine in January

The harvest happens when the temperature allows, often at three in the morning. We plan a winter journey around that window and the festivals that follow it.

The heritage town

A main street built for walking, with a theatre season, good coffee, and the river two blocks behind it. Toronto is an hour away.

A table at the winery

Dinner on the estate where the wine was made, then the drive back handled, so the evening can run as long as it wants to.

When to visit

Two seasons, two different journeys

From May to October the region runs on warm evenings, vineyard walks, and theatre. January is its other self: icewine harvest, festival weekends, and cellars that feel like the right place to be in the cold. Between the two, late September and early October bring the harvest and the autumn colour, which is the quietest good weather of the year.

Summer

Vineyard walks, long dinners, and the river at its best. Book the good tables early.

Autumn

Harvest, colour, and space. The connoisseur's window for a wine journey.

Winter

Icewine, festivals, and cellars. The only place in Canada to be in January.

For couples

A wine day, a long lunch, and an evening on the estate. Niagara-on-the-Lake is where we build the simplest romantic day in the portfolio, and the one guests most often ask to repeat.

For corporate groups

An hour from Toronto, which makes this the easiest single day retreat we design: a private cellar visit, a tasting for the team, and dinner on the estate, with transport handled from the city.

How we design this journey

Appointments first, then the town

We book the cellar appointments before we plan anything else, because the visit that makes the day is the one with a person rather than a pour. From there the shape is simple: two visits, one long lunch, and the evening wherever the light is best.

Niagara pairs naturally with Prince Edward County for a lakeshore journey of seven to ten days, and with the Rockies for guests who want wine and mountains in the same trip.

Questions we are asked

Frequently asked questions

Do you drive between the wineries on a Niagara private tour?

Yes. A private vehicle and driver stay with you for the day, which is the whole point of doing this region privately: nobody at the table has to count glasses, and the schedule bends when a visit runs long.

When is icewine season in Niagara-on-the-Lake?

The harvest is in January, picked by hand at night when the temperature falls below minus eight. The festivals follow the harvest, so a winter journey here is built around that window and confirmed against the growers' timing.

How far is Niagara-on-the-Lake from Toronto?

About an hour and a half by road from the city, which makes it the easiest wine region in Canada to build into a journey, and the simplest day retreat for a corporate group.
