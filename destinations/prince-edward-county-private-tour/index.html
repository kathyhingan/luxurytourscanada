The region

A county built on farm roads, not on coach routes.

Almost no luxury operator sells southern Ontario as a journey, which is exactly why it belongs in ours. The county sits an hour and a half east of the heritage waterfront at Kingston, and it holds Canada's youngest serious wine region, farm tables that fill up on a Friday, and the Sandbanks dunes, where the lake behaves like a coast.

The whole lakeshore from Kingston to Essex County has been ridden on a bicycle rather than driven past, and that changes what we recommend: the vineyard with the winemaker still pouring, the county road with the view, and the day built so nobody spends it in a car. Appointments do the work here the way they do in Niagara. The tasting with the person who made the wine, the table that is yours for the afternoon, and the dunes at the hour the day visitors have gone home.

Cellar appointments

Winemakers who still pour their own

The dunes at the mouth of the bay hold water that runs clear and shallow for a hundred metres, warm enough to swim by August. We take them late, after the day visitors have gone home.

The shoreline trail is flat, quiet, and exactly the right speed for this region. Your guide rides beside you, and the vineyard stops sit close enough that nobody counts kilometres.

Lunch within sight of the field

Point Pelee and the monarchs

The mainland's southernmost point is a May destination for migrating birds and a September one for monarchs. The ferry timetable decides the day, so we check it before we build anything around it.

Planning notes

What to know before you go

Getting there

Fly to Toronto, and the lakeshore runs east from there: about three hours to Kingston by road, and the county another ninety minutes along the same shore. Your guide and private vehicle collect you at the airport, so the journey starts at the first vineyard view rather than at a rental counter.

Best season

September and October bring the harvest and the colour within a fortnight of each other, the strongest window in the county. May and June are quiet and green, July and August belong to the dunes, and January pairs this region with the icewine harvest in Niagara for a winter journey.

The wine calendar

The county pours its newest vintages through the autumn, and the small wineries run on appointments rather than walk-in bars. We check the current vintage calendar before we book anything, and we keep a second appointment in reserve for any day the first one shifts.

Appointments and tables

Farm tables fill on summer Fridays and the winemaker visits book out through the harvest. We reserve both before we plan the rest, because the table and the tasting are the two things a schedule cannot bend around.

The bicycle days

The Waterfront Trail rides flat from Kingston west through the county, and the electric versions make the vineyard stops effortless. We ride it in the morning when the county roads are quietest, with the day's vehicle carrying the tasting and the picnic.

Pacing and care

A driver stays with the journey for its full length, which is what lets the days run at county speed. We recommend travel medical insurance for guests arriving from outside Canada and say so in writing, and the itinerary states the walking and riding at every stop honestly.

How we design this journey

A lakeshore built for short drives

Ontario looks flat on a map and is anything but on a schedule, so we cluster the days: Kingston and the islands first, then the county, then Niagara if the journey asks for it. Nobody spends three hours in a vehicle to reach a vineyard.

The whole road from Kingston to Essex has been ridden end to end, which is why we can tell you which town deserves two nights, which winery is worth an appointment, and which viewpoint is a photograph rather than an experience. We plan around the ferry timetable, the harvest dates, and the hour the dunes empty out.

Questions we are asked

Frequently asked questions

What is there to do in Prince Edward County?

Wine visits with the people who make it, farm tables, the Sandbanks dunes and the lake, and a county small enough to see by bicycle. Paired with Kingston, ninety minutes west, it becomes a proper journey with heritage, water, and food in it.

Is Prince Edward County good for a luxury Ontario wine tour?

It is the best-value wine region in the province for a private tour, and it is the one where winemakers will still sit down with you. Niagara-on-the-Lake has the older cellars and the icewine; the county has the appointments and the farm kitchens.

When is the best time for an Ontario lakeshore journey?

May to October, and September to October above all, when the harvest and the autumn colour overlap. Point Pelee is at its best in May for migrating birds and September for monarchs.
