The approach

A retreat is judged on execution, not intentions.

When a leadership team travels, one fear runs underneath everything: something going wrong on the organiser's watch, in front of the chief executive or the client. So we start from the opposite end to a leisure journey and work outward from execution. One point of contact, one plan, and a written contingency for weather, closures, and wildfire smoke at every stage.

What the group remembers afterwards is the part we do not compromise: a private dinner in a place no group has dined, a mountain morning that resets a room full of people, and a programme that leaves the team closer than any conference hotel could manage. The logistics exist so those moments can happen without anyone seeing the machinery.

Planning notes

What to know before you brief us

When to travel

June to early October for the Rockies, January to March for a coast programme built around storm watching. Wildfire season runs July to September, and it is planned around rather than hoped away.

Group size and shape

Six to thirty guests. Smaller leadership groups suit the coast, full-team retreats suit the Rockies, and the vehicle plan scales either way. One point of contact for the organiser, whatever the size.

Venues, honestly

We verify capacity, AV, and private-dining options before you commit, and we will tell you plainly what a room cannot do rather than promising a link we have not tested.

Gateways and transfers

Calgary, Vancouver, or Toronto, with private transfers and a transport manifest for every movement of the group, from the first airport pickup to the last drop-off.

The fallback

A fallback venue and a fallback sequence for weather, closures, or wildfire smoke are written into every programme before you travel, so a disruption changes the plan rather than the outcome.

After the retreat

Reflection built into the final evening rather than left to the flight home, and a debrief with the organiser while the week is still fresh.

How we design this programme

The days are written from the outcome backwards

We start with what the retreat is for: the decision the group needs to reach, the alignment it needs to feel, and the story it needs to tell afterwards. The programme is then written around those outcomes: the session that matters gets the best room and the freshest hours, the reset lands when attention sags, and the dinner comes at the point the group is ready for it.

Every one of those days has been run here before, in the cold months as well as the warm ones. That is the standard the programme is built to, and the reason the week ends with the team closer than any conference hotel could manage.

Questions we are asked

Frequently asked questions

What group sizes can you handle for a corporate retreat in Canada?

Six to thirty guests, across multi-day mountain and coastal retreats. Banff and Lake Louise handle larger groups comfortably, and the BC coast suits smaller leadership groups where the point is space rather than scale.

What happens if weather or wildfire smoke disrupts the programme?

We write a fallback venue and a fallback sequence into every programme before you travel, so a disruption changes the plan rather than the outcome. Wildfire season runs July to September, and it is planned around rather than hoped away.

Do you support hybrid sessions for remote attendees?

Yes. AV support is arranged for hybrid sessions and tested before the group arrives. We will tell you plainly what the venue can and cannot do rather than promising a link we have not verified.
