The approach

Family travel runs on rhythm, not distance.

The best family journey is not the one that sees the most. It is the one nobody has to recover from. We build each day around the children's hours: the drive that fits between a nap and a meal, the walk that ends while everyone still wants more, and the afternoon left open so the pool or the beach can win.

Everything children need is sourced before you arrive, from the right car seat in every vehicle to a cot, a high chair, and wetsuits for the water days. Guides who work with children lead those days, long legs get a stop written into them, and wildlife briefings happen before the walk rather than during it. Our own family does not travel on client journeys: local guides and vetted childminders handle the family-facing details on the ground.

Planning notes

What to know before you go

When to travel

Late June to early October for the Rockies, May to October for the coast and the lakeshore. July and August are warmest and busiest, which is why we start early and finish before the crowds arrive.

How long

Seven to ten days for one region with children, done slowly. Two regions only if the children are older, and never more than one long drive in a day.

Where to base

Two bases at most, and for young children one. Unpacking once matters more than adding a destination, and a lodge with room to run beats a list of towns.

The ages

Toddlers suit the Gulf Islands and the lakeshore. Six and up open up the Rockies properly. Tell us the ages and we will tell you honestly where the journey works.

The water

Every lake, pool, and beach day is guide-supervised, with wetsuits and child-sized safety gear, and a plan for the water that is simply too cold to swim.

If someone falls ill

A sick-day fallback is written into the itinerary before you fly, with a route to care and a plan for the days the first one is missed. Children are children.

How we design this journey

The days are written around the children's hours

We build each day around the two or three moments that make it worth the drive, then fill in around them: the flat water of the morning before the afternoon wind, the farm stand that holds a five year old for an hour, and the viewpoint that lands exactly where a leg stretch is needed.

Every one of those has been run here before, with children of our own and with families on the road, in the cold months as well as the warm ones. That is the standard the route is built to, and the reason a family journey here is shorter than it looks on paper and calmer than you expect on the day.

Questions we are asked

Frequently asked questions

What is the best Canada itinerary with a toddler?

Short distances, one base, and a gentle rhythm. Salt Spring Island and the Ontario lakeshore work better for toddlers than the Rockies, because the drives are fifteen minutes rather than three hours. If the Rockies are the dream, we build it around Jasper with nap-timed drives and a rest in the middle of each day.

Do you provide car seats for children in Canada?

Yes. Child seats are required by law, and we pre-source the correct seats for each child in every vehicle, fitted before you arrive. It is the first line of our pre-arrival checklist and not something you need to bring.

Is it safe to travel in bear country with children?

It is, with the right briefings. Wildlife is viewed at distance and guide-led, never approached, and every walk starts with a short briefing pitched at the children. Bear spray, group positioning, and route choice are the guide's job, and they are done every day.
