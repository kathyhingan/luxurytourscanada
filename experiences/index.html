What the segment changes

Same country, different rules.

Departure times

Retiree journeys hold a half past eight floor. Family journeys bend around naps. Everyone else starts when the light is best, which in June can be very early.

Driving days

The Rockies forgive a long drive and the parkway is the point of the day. The lakeshore never needs one. Family days are capped shorter, and retreat agendas keep transfers off them.

Activity level

The walking is stated honestly at every stop, from the steps at a canyon to a flat vineyard morning, and altitude notes are given in advance, not discovered on the day.

Tables and rooms

Honeymoons get the room and the table chosen first. Families get connecting rooms and early seatings. Retreats get one invoice and one point of contact.

Weather calls

Every outdoor day carries a second option, and a proposal carries a second location and a second time window, because mountain weather turns by the hour.

Questions we are asked

Frequently asked questions

How do you decide which shape of journey suits us?

By asking a handful of questions about who is travelling, the season, the pace, and what would make the trip worth it. Most parties land in one segment clearly. Some sit between two, usually independent traveller and professional on their yearly holiday, and we design for both.

Do you run group departures we can join?

No. Every journey is private to your party, with a maximum of eight guests. That is not a positioning statement, it is how the itinerary works: private vehicles, private boats, and days built around one group's pace.

Can you combine two segments in one journey?

Often, and it is usually families with retirees travelling together, or a corporate group extending into a private weekend. We design the operational rules for the stricter of the two segments and apply them throughout.
