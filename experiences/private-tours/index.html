Private Tours Canada: A Canada the Highway Does Not Show You

You have done Banff once, or you know it from a hundred photographs, and you are not going to see it from a bus seat. Good. Neither are we.

Plan My Private Journey

Independent travellers research deeply, dislike group tours, and find most luxury marketing patronising. So here is the plain version: we go where the coaches cannot, we time the famous places for the hours nobody else uses, and we name the places instead of describing the scenery.

What you are paying for is access and judgement. Knowing that Tofino is best in the shoulder and the outer waters, that Jasper beats Banff for a private base, that the Icefields Parkway needs a full tank before you start, and that the pull-off with the view is not the pull-off with the sign.

What we do differently

Three things that change the journey

The famous places at the quiet hours

Lake Louise at sunrise, Maligne Lake before the day visitors, Tofino's beaches before eight. Same places, entirely different experience.

Routes that skip the queue

Yoho instead of Banff for the quiet valley. The outer sound instead of the beach car park. The county road instead of the highway.

A guide who has done it many times

Not a driver with a script. Somebody who knows the road, the weather, the wildlife distances, and when to change the plan.

Questions we are asked

Frequently asked questions

What makes a private tour different from a small group tour?

The itinerary belongs to your party. There is no fixed departure, no other guests, and no schedule to fit around. Days start when you want them to, the route bends when something is worth staying for, and the vehicle and guide stay with you throughout.

Do you use coaches or vans for private tours in Canada?

Private vehicles with a guide, sized to the party, with child seats where they are needed. Coaches appear only if a corporate group explicitly wants one, and even then the days are built to avoid the places coaches queue.

Can you build a Canada tour around a specific interest, like wildlife or wine?

Yes, and it usually improves the journey. Wildlife shapes the Rockies and the BC coast. Wine shapes the Ontario lakeshore. Photography, hiking, and dark skies are all strong threads to pull through a route, and each one changes which months we recommend.
