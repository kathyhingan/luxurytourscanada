The approach

What you are paying for is access and judgement.

Independent travellers research deeply, dislike group tours, and find most travel marketing patronising. So here is the plain version: we go where the coaches cannot, we time the famous places for the hours nobody else uses, and we name the places instead of describing the scenery.

Planning notes

What to know before you go

When to travel

Late June to early October for the Rockies. May and June, then September and October, for the BC coast. May to October for the Ontario lakeshore. Tell us your month and we will tell you honestly which region suits it.

How long

Seven to fourteen days for one region done properly. Eighteen to twenty-one days to cross from the Pacific coast to the mountains and on to the lakeshore. A rushed Canada is a wasted one.

The vehicle

A private vehicle with a guide, sized to the party, with child seats where they are needed. Coaches appear only if a corporate group explicitly asks for one.

Water days

Boat and floatplane days are weather dependent. Your guide checks the marine forecast the evening before, and a fallback day is written into the plan before you fly.

The walking

Activity is stated honestly at every stop, from the steps at a canyon to a flat vineyard morning, and altitude notes come in advance rather than on the day.

What it includes

Private transfers throughout, a dedicated guide, park passes and entrance fees, wildlife briefings before every walk, and a 24-hour contact for the whole journey.

  • All private ground transfers, airport to lodge and between destinations
  • Domestic flights and floatplane transfers where the route calls for them
  • Private boat, canoe, and crew on every water day
  • Daily breakfast, plus the lunches and dinners noted in your itinerary
  • National and provincial park passes and all entrance fees
  • A single point of contact for changes, and a 24-hour line while you are in the country

Not included: international flights, alcohol and room charges, personal shopping, activities added outside the plan, travel medical insurance, eTA fees where required, and gratuities.

How we design this journey

The plan is written around the hours that matter

We build the day around the two or three moments that make it worth the drive, then fill in around them: the boat that leaves the harbour before the fleet, the cellar where the winemaker still pours, the walk into the old growth at the hour the light comes through the canopy.

Every one of those has been done here first, in the cold months as well as the warm ones, and in both directions. That is the standard the route is built to, and the reason a private journey here is shorter than it looks on paper and fuller than you expect on the day.

Questions we are asked

Frequently asked questions

What makes a private tour different from a small group tour?

The itinerary belongs to your party. There is no fixed departure, no other guests, and no schedule to fit around. Days start when you want them to, the route bends when something is worth staying for, and the vehicle and guide stay with you throughout.

Do you use coaches or vans for private tours in Canada?

Private vehicles with a guide, sized to the party, with child seats where they are needed. Coaches appear only if a corporate group explicitly wants one, and even then the days are built to avoid the places coaches queue.

Can you build a Canada tour around a specific interest, like wildlife or wine?

Yes, and it usually improves the journey. Wildlife shapes the Rockies and the BC coast. Wine shapes the Ontario lakeshore. Photography, hiking, and dark skies are all strong threads to pull through a route, and each one changes which months we recommend.
