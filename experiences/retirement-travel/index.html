The approach

The journey you waited decades for deserves patience in the design.

We design journeys for retirees as a core part of what we do, and what we have learned is that this is rarely just a trip. It is often the one that has been imagined through working years, talked about over dinners, and saved for carefully. That changes how we plan it: the pace, the properties, the sequence, everything built around comfort, familiarity, and the complete absence of rushing.

In practice that means one base at a time, short drives, the same vehicle and the same guide wherever the route allows, and an afternoon rest written into most days. Altitude is stated honestly, the nearest hospital or clinic is named for every destination, and if a day needs to change on the road, it changes. Nothing is fixed that does not have to be.

Planning notes

What to know before you go

When to travel

Late June to early October for the Rockies, May to October for the coast and the lakeshore. September suits this journey especially: warm roads, thin crowds, and the elk season in Jasper.

How long

Eight to twelve days for one region done slowly. Fewer bases and longer stays beat a list of destinations, at this pace more than any other.

Where to base

One base at a time, at the finest available property in each location. Unpacking once is part of the comfort, and a lodge with a view is on the programme, not an extra.

Altitude, honestly

Lake Louise sits at about 1,600 metres, so the first day there is built around a short walk and a long view. Every day names its altitude and its terrain in writing.

The rest

An afternoon rest is written into most days, not squeezed in around them. If any day needs to change on the road, we change it. Nothing is fixed that does not have to be.

If someone falls ill

A sick-day fallback is written into the itinerary before you fly, with the nearest clinic named and a plan for the days the first one is missed. Travel medical insurance is essential.

  • Pace-adjusted scheduling: no departures before 8:30am, with an afternoon rest built in
  • An accessibility briefing for each destination: walking distances, terrain, steps, and altitude
  • Accommodation at the finest available property in each location, confirmed after our call
  • The same vehicle and the same guide wherever the route allows

Questions we are asked

Frequently asked questions

Do your Canada tours involve early starts?

No departures before half past eight, and we mean it. Where wildlife is better at dawn, we move the outing to late afternoon or evening rather than moving your alarm, which is a better wildlife hour anyway once the day visitors have gone.

How do you handle altitude and mobility in the Rockies?

We state altitude honestly, build a gentle first day, and keep the walking optional and short, with the vehicle close by. Lake Louise sits at about 1,600 metres, so the first day there is designed around a short walk and a long view. Every itinerary carries walking distances, terrain, and any steps for each day.

Is travel medical insurance really necessary for Canada?

Yes. Canada's public healthcare does not cover visitors, and treatment without insurance is expensive. We recommend it in every proposal and treat it as essential for retirees. We will point you to providers, though the policy is yours to choose.
