The Salt Spring Island Saturday Market: A Summer Guide

By Kathy Hingan, Luxury Tours Canada. Publishing November 2026. Eight minutes to read.

Begin Your Journey

The Saturday market is the island's clock. By nine in the morning the park in the middle of Ganges is full: tables of vegetables still wet from the ground, bread in paper bags, cheese, honey, woodwork, paintings that came straight from the studio. Everything on the tables was made, baked, or grown by the person standing behind it, and that rule is the reason the market has run since the 1970s.

Summer is the market at full strength. The season runs every Saturday from early April to the end of October, and from the last Saturday of June to the Labour Day weekend the stalls stay up an hour longer, until four. This guide is about making a market Saturday the anchor of an island day, because that is what the island itself does.

What makes the Salt Spring Island Saturday Market different?

The rule is the difference. Most markets allow resellers; this one does not. The make, bake, or grow standard means the jam, the glaze on the pottery, and the salad greens all come from the same hands you are buying from, and a question about any of it gets answered by the maker. For a visitor it is the fastest hour of local contact the island gives.

The practical details matter. The market sits in Centennial Park in the village of Ganges, with parking tight on summer Saturdays, so we build the morning to arrive before nine and walk in from the harbour. Dogs stay out of the park by bylaw, which surprises visitors, and a daycare for them runs through the summer months. Bring a bag, and eat breakfast first: the baking goes early.

Everything sold in the park was made, baked, or grown by the hand that sells it.

When to go, and how a market Saturday fits a journey

The season runs from the first Saturday of April to the last of October, nine in the morning to three in the shoulder months and to four in high season. High season runs from the last Saturday of June to the Labour Day weekend. In December the market returns for the first three Saturdays, ten to three, which is a smaller, woodsmoke version of the same thing and worth knowing about if winter is your season on the coast.

A market morning pairs naturally with the rest of the island: the studios and farms that open through the week, the viewpoint road up Mount Maxwell, a slow afternoon on the southern end at Ruckle Park, and a ferry home that leaves the day unhurried.

One operational note worth having: summer ferries to Salt Spring run on sailings that fill, so the day is built around the boat times first and the market hours second. That ordering is most of what a private plan buys you here.

For couples

Arrive on the Friday, take the market slowly on Saturday morning, and give the afternoon to the vineyards and the viewpoint. The island is small, and a day without a schedule suits it.

For families

The market is genuinely good with children: room to move, food they will actually eat, and makers who will talk to them. Keep the morning to the park, swim in the afternoon, and let the ferry be the last event of the day rather than the race.

What a private guide changes about a market day

Timing, mostly, and the day built around it. The ferry sailings, the parking, the hour the baking goes, the farms and studios that answer when you arrive: a private day chains these so the market is the anchor and not the queue. We build the day around the boat times, put the market first, and leave the afternoon open enough to follow whatever the makers point you toward.

That is the difference between visiting the market and having a Salt Spring Saturday.

Questions we are asked

Frequently asked questions

When does the Salt Spring Island Saturday Market run?

Every Saturday from the first Saturday of April to the last of October. Hours are nine to three in the shoulder months and nine to four in high season, from the last Saturday of June to the Labour Day weekend. The market also returns for the first three Saturdays of December, ten to three.

Can anything be bought at the market that was not made on the island?

No. The market has run on a make, bake, or grow rule since the 1970s: everything sold is made, baked, or grown by the vendor behind the table. That rule is why the produce, the baking, and the craft are all traceable to a person, and why the market is worth planning a morning around.

Is a market day worth building a journey around?

In summer, yes. Pair the morning in Ganges with the farms, studios, and viewpoints that open through the afternoon, and build the day around the ferry sailings rather than the market hours. With the boats timed first, the market becomes the anchor of the island day instead of a stop on it.
